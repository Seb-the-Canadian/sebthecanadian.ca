We use AI for knowledge-work; so, who controls the infrastructure, and what does that mean for shared reality?

When knowledge platforms become centralized under corporate or political control, we don't just risk misinformation. We risk losing the epistemic commons that makes collective sense-making possible.

Wikipedia works because it's transparent (edit history visible), community-governed (not one person's agenda), accountable (volunteers challenge each other), and imperfect but improvable.

AI knowledge platforms controlled by single entities skip all of that. No transparency. No community oversight. Just algorithmic authority that sounds credible.

Fundamentally, the architecture matters.

We need knowledge systems that preserve both individual sovereignty (you control your data) and collaborative intelligence (we can think together). Infrastructure that resists capture--whether by corporations, governments, or ideologues.

This means:  
- Federation over centralization  
- Trust networks over algorithmic gatekeepers  
- Portable data over platform lock-in  
- Structural literacy, not just media literacy

I believe we're at a choice point. The next decade of knowledge infrastructure will either entrench power or distribute it.
